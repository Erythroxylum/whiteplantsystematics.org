Integrative plant systematics.

We study plant diversity across scales, combining fieldwork, herbarium collections, genomics, spectral phenomics, and conservation. Based at Texas A&M University and connected to the S.M. Tracy Herbarium, our work uses botanical collections as infrastructure for discovery, training, and stewardship.

Current directions

ErythroxylaceaeCoca, domestication, and plant variationIntegrative systematics, genomics, morphology, chemistry, and policy-relevant botanical knowledge.

We study the coca family to understand species limits, adaptation, domestication, biogeography, and the distinction between plant diversity and simplified policy categories.

Herbarium spectral biologyPhenomic datasets from preserved specimensLeaf reflectance spectroscopy, trait prediction, taxonomic modeling, and community standards through IHerbSpec.

We develop and apply spectral methods that make herbarium specimens useful for studying plant form and function across time, space, and lineages.
